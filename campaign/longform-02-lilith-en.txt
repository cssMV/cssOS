Before Eve, there was Lilith.

Same day. Same dust. The oldest telling says she was made from the earth exactly as Adam was — not from his rib, not from his side, not from anything that belonged to him. Equal material. Equal hour.

Then Adam told her to lie beneath him.

She answered: we were made from the same earth, so why should I be under you?

He would not hear it. So she spoke the ineffable Name, rose into the air, and walked out of the garden. She stole nothing. She killed no one. She betrayed no one. She left a marriage she had never agreed to.

For that, three thousand years of literature made her a demon.

She becomes the night-witch, the strangler of newborns, the succubus blamed for what sleeping men dream. From Mesopotamia to medieval Europe, mothers hung her name on amulets over their cradles to keep her out. The woman who said "no" one time became the reason a child dies in the night.

And Adam? Adam is still the father of mankind.

This is the pattern, and it is older than any one civilization: men ruin nations; women carry the name for it.

Nineveh, Chang'an, Troy, Thebes — the men held the army, the treasury, the throne and the decision. And when it all burned, the record needed a face to hang the fire on. It never chose the man holding the seal. It chose the woman standing next to him.
