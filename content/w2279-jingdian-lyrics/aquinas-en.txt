[Style: Medieval Latin sacred music, Gregorian chant opening into two-part organum, male schola with organ drone, Dominican plainsong, stone-vault reverberation, very slow]
[Vocal: male schola cantorum in unison, Latin-rite diction, straight tone, no vibrato, one solo cantor on the verses]
[Language: English. One line of his own Latin hymn in the Chorus, one archaic Latin charm in the Bridge.]

[Verse 1: Monte Cassino, Age Five]
They gave me to the abbey when I was five,
a count's ninth son, an oblate on the hill.
I learned the hours before I learned to ride.
In that old school each book was like a star,
and every star was fixed, and I was not.

[Verse 2: Cologne, Under Albert]
At Naples I put on the black and white
and my own brothers held me for a year.
In Cologne the philosopher's voice was like
thunder across the lecture hall, and clear:
this ox, he said, will bellow for the world.

[Chorus 1: Pange Lingua]
Pange, lingua, gloriosi corporis mysterium —
sing, tongue, the mystery of the glorious body.
The psalms come back along the ear and run;
truth leaps along the fingers of the godly.
Faith and reason walking. Neither blind.

[Verse 3: The Chair at Paris, 1256]
The light of Paris on the rue du Fouarre,
the halls on straw, the students on the floor.
Two chairs for the Dominicans, and the war
with the secular masters at the door.
In the sea of truth I will swim free.

[Verse 4: The Objections Come First]
I always set the strongest case against me
at the head of every question, word for word,
so fairly that the heretics could sign it.
Then I answered. That was the whole method:
never beat an argument you softened.

[Bridge: Huat Hanat Huat]
[Archaic Latin incantation, Cato, De Agri Cultura 160, the oldest recorded Latin charm]
Huat hanat huat, ista pista sista,
damiabo damnaustra — a charm to set a limb.
Older than the Church, older than the schools,
this was the Latin when the Latin whispered.
It is the same tongue. I only turned it up.

[Chorus 3: Pange Lingua]
Pange, lingua, gloriosi corporis mysterium —
sing, tongue, the mystery of the glorious body.

[Chorus 4: All of It Is Straw]
Naples, the sixth of December, seventy-three:
something at Mass, and I would write no more.
Omnia quae scripsi videntur mihi paleae —
all I have written seems like straw to me.
Reginald begged. I said: I cannot. Stop.

[Outro: Fossanova, 7 March 1274]
A mule, a doorpost, and a Cistercian bed
on the road to Lyon that I did not finish.
They asked for a commentary on the Song
and I gave them one, and then the voice went thin.
In the old school I was weaving. Let it stand.
