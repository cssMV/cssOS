[Style: Modern classical chamber, solo piano with restrained strings, Polish folk cadence under a French salon surface, sparse and unsentimental, andante tranquillo]
[Vocal: mezzo-soprano lead, plain and level, a small female choir answering in unison, no ornament]
[Language: English. One line of the French original in the Chorus, one line of the oldest Polish hymn in the Bridge.]

[Verse 1: The Flying University, Warsaw]
A rented room, a different room each week,
because the Tsar would not let women learn.
We read in Polish. We were taught to speak
in a language they had ruled it best to burn.
That was the first laboratory I had.

[Verse 2: The Shed on Rue Lhomond]
A shed with a cracked roof and a dirt floor,
eight tonnes of pitchblende stirred with an iron rod.
Four years of boiling ore to get one more
decigram of something nobody had.
At night the bottles glowed. We went to look.

[Chorus 1: Nothing Is to Be Feared]
Rien dans la vie n'est a craindre, mon ami,
quand on le comprend: nothing in this life
is to be feared, it is to be understood.
Not the dark, not the dose, not the knife.
Understand it. Then it is not fear.

[Verse 3: Polonium, 1898]
I named the first one for a country gone,
erased from every map in Europe then.
Poland was not a state. I put it on
the periodic table. It is there again.
Radium came after. Both of them were ours.

[Verse 4: Rue Dauphine, 19 April 1906]
A wet street and a horse and a dray's wheel,
and Pierre went under it, and that was all.
They offered me his chair. I said I will
take it, and I took it, and the hall
was full of men who had not wanted me.

[Chorus 2: Nothing Is to Be Feared]
Rien dans la vie n'est a craindre, mon ami,
quand on le comprend: nothing in this life
is to be feared, it is to be understood.
Not the dark, not the dose, not the knife.
Understand it. Then it is not fear.

[Bridge: Bogurodzica — the oldest hymn in our tongue]
[Oldest Polish incantation-hymn, the Bogurodzica, sung by the knights at Grunwald]
Bogurodzica dziewica, Bogiem slawiena Maryja,
u twego Syna Gospodzina matko zwolena, Maryja.
Older than the partitions, older than
the language they forbade us in the schools:
the knights sang this. I was taught it as a child.

[Chorus 3: Nothing Is to Be Feared]
Rien dans la vie n'est a craindre, mon ami,
quand on le comprend: nothing in this life
is to be feared, it is to be understood.
Not the dark, not the dose, not the knife.
Understand it. Then it is not fear.

[Chorus 4: The Little Curies, 1914]
I took the radium to the front in vans
and taught two hundred women how to read
a shadow of a bullet in the hands
of a boy from Lille. That was the only creed
I ever had: the thing is to be used.

[Outro: Sancellemoz, 4 July 1934]
The notebooks are still warm. They always will be —
they keep them in a lead box in Paris.
My blood gave out. The marrow would not fill.
I had two Nobels and a cough, and this:
no one is afraid of what they understand.
