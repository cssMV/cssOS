[Style: Modern classical, solo piano with a string quartet entering late, Polish mazurka rhythm buried under a French funeral march, restrained and gathering, andante]
[Vocal: mezzo-soprano lead, level and unsentimental, a small female choir in unison on the choruses, no ornament]
[Language: English. One line of her own French in the Chorus, one line of the oldest written Polish in the Bridge.]

[Verse 1: The Academy Votes, January 1911]
They put me up for the Academy of Sciences
and I lost by two votes to a man named Branly.
The debate was whether a woman could be elected
and whether a Pole was French. They settled it plainly:
she is a foreigner, and she is a woman. No.

[Verse 2: November 1911 — the Letters in the Paper]
Then a newspaper printed private letters
between me and a married former student
of my husband's, and a crowd came to the house
and my daughters went out the back. Prudent
men advised me to decline the second prize.

[Chorus 1: What Remains to Be Done]
On ne remarque jamais ce qui a été fait;
on ne peut voir que ce qui reste à faire —
one never notices what has been done,
one only sees what still remains. So there:
I went to Stockholm. I collected it myself.

[Verse 3: The Second Nobel, 10 December 1911]
Chemistry, this time, and in my own name,
for isolating radium as a metal.
No one else has two in two sciences.
I stood up in the hall and read the lecture
and said nothing at all about the papers.

[Verse 4: Irene, and the Vans]
My daughter was seventeen at the front with me
reading plates in a tent at Hoogstade.
In 1935 she took the prize in chemistry
and I had been a year in the ground.
Eve wrote the book. Irene did the work.

[Chorus 2: What Remains to Be Done]
On ne remarque jamais ce qui a été fait;

[Bridge: Day, Ut Ia Pobrusa]
[The oldest written sentence in Polish, the Book of Henrykow, 1270]
Day, ut ia pobrusa, a ti poziwai —
let me grind a while, and you take rest.
A man said that to his wife at the millstone.
It is the first Polish anyone wrote down,
and I stirred eight tonnes of ore with an iron rod.

[Chorus 3: What Remains to Be Done]
On ne remarque jamais ce qui a été fait;
on ne peut voir que ce qui reste à faire —
one never notices what has been done,
one only sees what still remains. So there:
I went to Stockholm. I collected it myself.

[Chorus 4: Never Patented]
We never patented the isolation process.
Pierre said it would be contrary to the spirit
of science, and I agreed, and so the radium
industry grew up in Belgium and we hired it
by the gram at a price we could not pay.

[Outro: The Pantheon, 20 April 1995]
Sixty-one years later they moved us both
under the dome, the fourth and fifth beneath it,
and I was the first woman put there on her merit
and not as somebody's wife. Lead-lined, both coffins.
The notebooks stay in the box. Still counting down.
