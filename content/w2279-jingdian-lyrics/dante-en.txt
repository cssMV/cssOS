[Style: Italian sacred polyphony, Gregorian chant opening into Renaissance choir, pipe organ and low strings, cathedral reverberation, grave and ascending, largo]
[Vocal: tenor cantor answered by a full mixed choir, Latin-rite diction, straight tone]
[Language: English. One line of the Italian original in the Chorus, one line of the oldest Italian in the Bridge.]

[Verse 1: Midway]
Midway along the road our life is set
I woke inside a wood without a path.
The night was that one night you don't forget;
the slope was there, the lion and the wrath,
and someone came who said: another way.

[Verse 2: The Gate]
Through me the way into the grieving town,
through me the way into eternal pain.
It says that on the lintel, going down.
Abandon every hope, you who remain.
I read it. And I went in anyway.

[Chorus 1: The Love That Moves the Sun]
L'amor che move il sole e l'altre stelle —
the love that moves the sun and the other stars.
Nine heavens turning, and the one who tells
the turning what to do is all there was.
And from there we came out to see the stars.

[Verse 3: Exile, 1302]
They sentenced me in absence, burned my name,
and gave my house in Florence to a friend
of Corso Donati. I never came
back up the Arno. Every road would bend
to someone else's stairs, and someone's bread.

[Verse 4: Beatrice on the Terrace]
A girl in Florence, crimson, nine years old;
and then the bridge, the greeting, and the death.
I put her at the top of Paradise and told
the whole of Christendom to hold its breath.
She looked at me and did not say my name.

[Chorus 2: The Love That Moves the Sun]

[Bridge: Se Pareba Boves — the oldest line in our tongue]
[Oldest Italian incantation-riddle, the Veronese Riddle, eighth or ninth century]
Se pareba boves, alba pratalia araba,
albo versorio teneba, negro semen seminaba.
He drove the oxen, ploughed the white fields white,
he held the white plough and he sowed black seed:
that was a man writing. That was already us.

[Chorus 3: The Love That Moves the Sun]
L'amor che move il sole e l'altre stelle —
the love that moves the sun and the other stars.
Nine heavens turning, and the one who tells
the turning what to do is all there was.
And from there we came out to see the stars.

[Chorus 4: In the Vernacular]
I wrote it in the language of the street,
not Latin, not the clerks', not for the schools —
in Tuscan, so a woman in her seat
by the fire could follow it. They called it rude.
It is the language now. It was a bet.

[Outro: Ravenna]
They would not give you back your city, ever;
Ravenna took the bones and kept them hid
in a wall when Florence sent for them. Forever
the city that condemned you wants the lid.
Sleep. The stars are the last word in each book.
