[Style: French Baroque chamber, viol consort and theorbo, slow sarabande, candlelit cabinet acoustics, measured and sceptical, andante]
[Vocal: tenor lead, intimate and spoken-close, a sparse choir entering only on the chorus, little vibrato]
[Language: English. One line of Latin and one of French in the Chorus, one line of the oldest French poem in the Bridge.]

[Verse 1: Neuburg, 10 November 1619]
Deep thinking goes through the dark like water goes.
Winter quarters, a stove-heated room,
one soldier with no battle and no clothes
to speak of, and a whole day in the gloom:
I had nothing to do but doubt everything.

[Verse 2: The Three Dreams]
In the dream at Neuburg I was blown by wind
against a chapel wall, and could not stand.
Then thunder, sparks. Then a dictionary, and
a book of poems opened in my hand
at a line that asked: which road of life is mine?

[Chorus 1: Cogito]
Dubito, ergo cogito, ergo sum —
je pense, donc je suis. The soul's own voice
goes up and carries to the sky and comes
back down as the one thing left me by the choice
to throw out everything. Wisdom's light. And this.

[Verse 3: The Fly on the Ceiling]
A fly in the corner of a plastered room:
how do I say exactly where it sits?
Two walls, a floor, three numbers, and you come
to any point in all the world that fits.
The beauty of the math is in the stars.

[Verse 4: Larvatus Prodeo]
I come forward masked — I wrote that as a boy
in a notebook nobody was meant to read.
I held the Galileo book with joy
and locked my own away. I would not bleed
for a proposition. I would live and write.

[Bridge: Buona Pulcella Fut Eulalia]
[Oldest French poem, the Sequence of Saint Eulalia, around 881]
Buona pulcella fut Eulalia,
bel auret corps, bellezour anima.
A good girl was Eulalia; fair was her body,
fairer her soul — that was already French,
the first of us to sing and not translate.

[Chorus 3: Cogito]
Dubito, ergo cogito, ergo sum —
je pense, donc je suis. The soul's own voice
goes up and carries to the sky and comes
back down as the one thing left me by the choice
to throw out everything. Wisdom's light. And this.

[Chorus 4: Wandering the Universe]
I wander in the universe and ask
what lies ahead; the road has no last mile.
Truth is waiting and in no great rush.
The questions are not finished. All the while
the method is the only thing I own.

[Outro: Stockholm, 11 February 1650]
A queen who wanted lessons at five in the morning,
a Swedish winter, and a lung that filled.
When the dream broke there was a scent of thinking
left in the room, and a long argument stilled.
They took the skull to Paris. Not the rest.
