[Style: Cinematic synthwave, warm analogue pads and arpeggiated bass, gated reverb snare, city-at-dawn motion, nocturnal and tender, mid-tempo 100 BPM]
[Vocal: baritone lead, close and conversational over the synths, a soft mixed choir on the choruses, little vibrato]
[Language: English. One line of Old English incantation in the Bridge.]

[Verse 1: Six Fifteen]
Six fifteen and the street is mine and theirs,
a key in every lockbox down the block.
Marnie first, then Otis, then the pair
of whippets on the ninth who hate the clock.
Nobody here has met me. All the dogs have.

[Verse 2: Eleven Leashes]
Eleven leashes braided in one hand,
and every one of them a different weight.
The big one pulls, the old one wants to stand
and read the hedge for twenty minutes straight.
You learn a street by what a dog stops at.

[Chorus 1: They Also Serve]
They also serve who only stand and wait —
a blind man wrote that, and he meant himself.
I wait outside a hundred closing gates.
The city runs on people on the shelf
who hold the thing the city has let go.

[Verse 3: What the Owners Don't See]
They text me from a meeting: how's my boy?
I send a photograph: he's in the grass.
They do not know he limps on the right, or
that he stands at the door an hour after I pass.
I know their dogs better than they do. That's the job.

[Verse 4: The Rain Does Not Count]
Rain does not cancel. Snow does not cancel.
The dog does not know that the sky is wrong.
I own one coat that has gone through four winters
and a pair of boots that will not last this long.
Four dollars a walk less than it should be.

[Chorus 2: They Also Serve]
They also serve who only stand and wait —

I wait outside a hundred closing gates.

[Bridge: Find That Feoh and Fere That Feoh]
[Old English incantation, the charm against cattle-theft, eleventh-century manuscript]
Garmund, godes thegen,
find thaet feoh and fere thaet feoh.
Garmund, servant of God, find the beasts
and bring the beasts back home — a thousand years
and the oldest English spell is still my job.

[Chorus 3: They Also Serve]
They also serve who only stand and wait —
a blind man wrote that, and he meant himself.
I wait outside a hundred closing gates.
The city runs on people on the shelf
who hold the thing the city has let go.

[Chorus 4: The Hour Before the City]
There is one hour before the city starts
when the light comes up grey along the river
and the dogs go quiet and the traffic parts
and nothing is required of me whatever.
That hour is paid for in eleven leashes.

[Outro: Returning Them]
One at a time I put them back inside.
The door clicks. The dog turns round to look.
I say the same four words I always say,
go on, good, go on, and close the book,
and walk to the next street with my hands empty.
