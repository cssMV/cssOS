[Style: Cinematic synthwave, slow-decay pads and a single sequenced bassline, no drums until the fourth chorus, dusk and streetlight, elegiac and spacious, slow 78 BPM]
[Vocal: baritone lead, quiet and unhurried, a wordless mixed choir rising late, no ornament]
[Language: English. One line of Old English incantation in the Bridge.]

[Verse 1: He Used to Do the Whole Loop]
He used to do the whole loop in the dark,
past the depot, down the towpath, round the lock.
Last spring he stopped halfway and sat down
and looked at me the way a dog will look
when he has something true to tell you. So I sat.

[Verse 2: Shorter Every Month]
We go to the corner now. That is the walk.
Four minutes out, a long smell at the post,
four minutes back. He plans it. He takes stock.
His owner works until the light is lost
and does not see how carefully he does it.

[Chorus 1: Promises to Keep]
The woods are lovely, dark and deep,
but I have promises to keep —
and miles to go, and one old dog to carry
up two flights when the stairs are not his friend.
Whoever waits at home, I am the one who came.

[Verse 3: I Am the One Who Is There]
A dog's whole day is the ten minutes with me.
I am not family. I am not a vet.
I am the handle on the door, the key,
the person in between what he can get
and what he needs. There is no word for that.

[Verse 4: The Last Week]
On Tuesday he would not stand up for the lead.
I carried him to the grass and set him down
and he put his face into it, and he breathed,
and that was the walk. I called the owner. She drove down
from somewhere two hours away, and got there.

[Bridge: Ne Forstolen Ne Forholen]
[Old English incantation, the charm for lost property, eleventh-century manuscript]
Ne forstolen ne forholen nawiht,
thaes the ic age.
Let nothing I have be stolen or hidden from me —
the oldest English spell is a man in a doorway
asking to keep what was put in his hands.

[Chorus 3: Promises to Keep]
The woods are lovely, dark and deep,
but I have promises to keep —
and miles to go, and one old dog to carry
up two flights when the stairs are not his friend.
Whoever waits at home, I am the one who came.

[Chorus 4: The Lockbox Is Still There]
The lockbox is still screwed beside the door
with the same four numbers nobody has changed.
I walked past it all summer. I don't any more.
Someone will move in. The code will be arranged.
A dog's name does not come off a street for years.

[Outro: Go On, Good, Go On]
I have eleven others and they're fine.
The whippets, Marnie, Otis, all of them.
I take the towpath now and do the whole line
the long way round, past the depot and the lock,
and say it anyway: go on, good, go on.
