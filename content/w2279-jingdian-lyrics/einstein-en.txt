[Style: Modern classical chamber, solo violin over muted strings and piano, slow passacaglia, Central European and interior, contemplative, adagio sostenuto]
[Vocal: baritone lead, close and conversational, small wordless choir on the choruses, almost no vibrato]
[Language: English. One line of the German original in the Chorus, one line of ancient Hebrew blessing in the Bridge.]

[Verse 1: The Patent Clerk, Bern, 1905]
Third class, Bern, a desk of other men's machines,
a stack of claims for better railway clocks.
Between two stamps I set two clocks between
two moving trains, and found the thing unlocks:
there is no now that everybody shares.

[Verse 2: The Violin in the Evening]
At night I take the violin and play
Mozart until the argument comes clear.
The bow does what the algebra won't say.
Elsa shuts the door. The notes stay here.
I think in music, and I check in math.

[Chorus 1: Subtle Is the Lord]
Raffiniert ist der Herrgott, aber boshaft ist er nicht —
subtle is the Lord, but He is not malicious.
He will not cheat you; He will hide the trick
one layer deeper than you thought was his.
And God, I still believe, does not play dice.

[Verse 3: Eclipse, 29 May 1919]
They photographed the sun gone black at noon
from Principe and Sobral, and the stars
had moved — a hair, an arcsecond, a spoon
of sky bent round the mass. The morning papers
had my name in them by Christmas. I was tired.

[Verse 4: The Letter, 2 August 1939]
I signed a letter I would not sign twice.
Szilard wrote it. I put my name below.
A single page, and then the whole world's price.
I was a pacifist with one hand slow.
The equation was never the mistake.

[Bridge: Yevarechecha — the oldest written blessing in our tongue]
[Ancient Hebrew incantation, the Priestly Blessing, Ketef Hinnom silver scrolls, seventh century BC]
Yevarechecha Adonai ve-yishmerecha,
ya'er Adonai panav eleycha vi-chuneka.
Older than the physics, older than the law,
this was the oldest writing of my people:
may the face turn toward you. May it shine.

[Chorus 4: I Want to Know His Thoughts]
I want to know the thoughts of God, I said;
the rest is detail. That was not a boast.
It was a working method, and it led
through thirty quiet years and almost lost
me everything. The field is still not joined.

[Outro: Princeton, April 1955]
I will go when I want to go. It is tasteless
to prolong a life artificially.
I have done my share. Burn what is left, and place
no stone, no name, no road, no plaque for me —
the ashes went into a river. Let them go.
