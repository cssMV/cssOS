[Style: German Baroque with mechanical percussion, harpsichord and chamber organ over a clockwork tick, two-voice canon, Hanover workshop acoustics, precise and bright, allegretto]
[Vocal: baritone lead with a two-part canon echoing him a beat late, Bach-cantata diction, no vibrato]
[Language: English. One Latin motto in the Chorus, one line of Old High German counting-charm in the Bridge.]

[Verse 1: The Stepped Reckoner, 1673]
It is unworthy of excellent men to lose
whole hours like slaves in the labour of a sum.
So I built brass: a drum with stepped grooves,
a carry that moves over when it should come,
and all four operations in one box.

[Verse 2: London Laughs Politely]
I showed it to the Royal Society
and it jammed on the carry, as it would
for thirty years and every clockmaker.
One machine, one craftsman, and no good
way to cut a gear true. It was right on paper.

[Chorus 1: Omnibus Ex Nihilo]
Omnibus ex nihilo ducendis sufficit unum —
to draw all things from nothing, one suffices.
Nothing and one. Write zero, and write one,
and every number in the world arises.
I asked them for a medal. They said no.

[Verse 3: Nothing and One]
Two marks, and I can write the whole of count:
the empty and the full, the night and day,
creation out of nothing in an amount
a child could check. Then double it away
and the places carry upward, two by two.

[Verse 4: Father Bouvet's Letter from Peking]
A Jesuit sent me a diagram of lines,
broken and whole, in sixty-four arrangements,
drawn by a Chinese sage four thousand years
before me — and it was my own invention.
I wept over a page I had not written.

[Chorus 2: Omnibus Ex Nihilo]

[Bridge: Gang Uz, Nesso]
[Old High German counting-charm, Pro Nessia, ninth-century manuscript]
Gang uz, nesso, mit niun nessinchlinon,
uz fonna marge in deo adra.
Go out, worm, with your nine little worms,
out from the marrow into the vein —
the oldest German charm I know is counting.

[Chorus 3: Omnibus Ex Nihilo]
Omnibus ex nihilo ducendis sufficit unum —
to draw all things from nothing, one suffices.
Nothing and one. Write zero, and write one,
and every number in the world arises.
I asked them for a medal. They said no.

[Chorus 4: A Machine That Reasons]
If we had the characters, I said, we could
settle a dispute the way we settle sums:
sit down, take up the pen, and say, let us
calculate — and one of us would be wrong
and both of us would know it in an hour.

[Outro: Three Centuries Late]
The brass machine sat broken in a loft
in Goettingen until the nineteen-hundreds.
The two marks went into a different box
with no gears in it at all, and they moved,
and everything you are holding runs on them.
