[Style: German high Baroque, chamber organ and strings, fugal counterpoint, walking bass, Lutheran chorale cadence, measured and luminous, andante maestoso]
[Vocal: baritone lead with a small four-part chorale answering, Bach-passion declamation, little vibrato]
[Language: English. One line of Old High German incantation in the Bridge, one Latin motto in the Chorus.]

[Verse 1: Under the Silent Sky]
Under the silent sky the stars stand thick
above the Leine and the ducal gate.
A clerk with too much work and one small trick
of notation sits and will not go to bed:
the night is long, and infinite, and short.

[Verse 2: Wandering the Ocean of Knowledge]
I wander in an ocean made of books —
law, mining, China, history, mathematics —
and hope burns in me like a lamp that looks
for one true alphabet beneath the statics,
one script in which all thinking could be done.

[Chorus 1: Calculemus]
Oh, the bright glory of the calculus:
the door of logic and of truth swings wide.
Nothing at all is ever without cause — thus
let us not argue, friend. Let us decide.
Calculemus. Let us calculate.

[Verse 3: Geometry and Living Force]
I turn the mystery of the world around:
geometry and living force entwined,
the curve made out of straight things finely ground,
the tangent and the quadrature combined,
and the long S I drew to mean a sum.

[Verse 4: The Quarrel with the Englishman]
Newton had it first and would not say;
I had it after and I wrote it best.
The Royal Society gave him the day,
and both of us were right, and both were pressed.
History kept the notation. Keep the sign.

[Chorus 2: Calculemus]

[Bridge: Ben Zi Bena — bone to bone]
[Old High German incantation, the Second Merseburg Charm, tenth-century manuscript]
Ben zi bena, bluot zi bluoda,
lid zi geliden, sose gelimida sin.
Bone to bone and blood to blood, and so
all things that are are jointed and agreed:
no single thing can move but all consent.

[Chorus 3: Calculemus]
Oh, the bright glory of the calculus:
the door of logic and of truth swings wide.
Nothing at all is ever without cause — thus
let us not argue, friend. Let us decide.
Calculemus. Let us calculate.

[Chorus 4: Nothing Without Reason]
In the long river of time, thought is a flame
that nothing drowns and nothing quite puts out;
it lights the corners of a coming age
that will not know your wig, your gout, your doubt.
All things are joined. And cause walks close behind.

[Outro: Hanover, Winter Rain]
They buried you with almost nobody there —
one clerk, one priest, one carriage, one cold rain.
Thank you, Leibniz. Everything you were
is in the mark that every schoolchild writes.
You lit all of this, and then you went out.
