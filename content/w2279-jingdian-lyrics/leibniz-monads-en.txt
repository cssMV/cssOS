[Style: German Baroque, harpsichord and viola da gamba, slow chaconne over a ground bass, chapel reverberation, contemplative, adagio]
[Vocal: tenor lead, single voice with sparse chorale echo, oratorio recitative, restrained]
[Language: English. One line of archaic Latin incantation in the Bridge.]

[Verse 1: A Preface Under Stars]
Under the quiet sky my thinking drifts
along beside the slow stars, and won't land.
The mind goes out from me in little rifts
and comes back holding almost nothing. And
the almost-nothing is the whole of it.

[Verse 2: The Nature of All Things]
I think upon the nature of all things:
what substance is, what makes a thing one thing.
The mystery of space and time, which brings
its own best answer only by withdrawing.
There is no empty room. There is no when.

[Chorus 1: Truth Like Scattered Stars]
Oh, truth is scattered out like glittering stars;
the seeking heart has never once stood still.
Cum Deus calculat, fit mundus — thus:
when God computes, and bends his thought to will,
a world comes out. We live inside the sum.

[Verse 3: The Monads Have No Windows]
The monads have no windows, none at all;
each one a little mirror of the whole.
Nothing comes in. Nothing goes through the wall.
And yet they are in concert, soul with soul,
because the score was written all at once.

[Verse 4: The Best of All Possible Worlds]
Of all the worlds that could have been let stand,
this one was chosen — not the kindest one,
the richest in its reasons, best in hand.
They laughed at me in Paris. Voltaire won
the century. The argument remains.

[Bridge: Enos Lases Iuvate]
[Archaic Latin incantation, the Carmen Arvale, oldest surviving Latin verse]
Enos Lases iuvate, enos Lases iuvate,
neve lue rue, Marmar, sins incurrere in pleoris.
Older than the schools, older than the proofs,
this was the Latin back when Latin prayed,
and all my demonstrations lean on it.

[Chorus 3: Truth Like Scattered Stars]

[Chorus 4: The Lighthouse of Philosophy]
The lighthouse of philosophy still turns
across an ocean nobody has crossed.
The sparks go up. Each separate spark burns
exactly once, and none of it is lost:
the sum of all of them is called a world.

[Outro: Let My Thought Be a River]
Let all my thinking run the way a river runs,
through wisdom's open country, loud and wide,
past Hanover, past Leipzig, past the sons
of men who never heard my name, and die
still holding, without knowing, what I signed.
