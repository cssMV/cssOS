[Style: German Baroque sacred, full chamber organ with low strings and a single oboe, Lutheran chorale harmonisation, church acoustics, argumentative then resigned, andante grave]
[Vocal: bass lead with a four-part chorale answering, oratorio declamation, straight tone]
[Language: English. One Latin principle in the Chorus, one line of Old High German charm in the Bridge.]

[Verse 1: Why Is There Anything Wrong]
A queen in Berlin asked me the old question:
if He is good and able, why the plague?
I wrote four hundred pages in French for her
and gave the thing a name nobody had —
theodicy. The trial of God. His case.

[Verse 2: The Only Book I Published]
Everything else I wrote stayed in a drawer
or went out in a letter to one man.
This one I printed: seventeen hundred ten,
in Amsterdam, in French, so it would scan
for a court and not a faculty of theology.

[Chorus 1: Nihil Est Sine Ratione]
Nihil est sine ratione — nothing is
without a reason, nothing stands alone.
Not the stone falling, not the suffering,
not the one arrangement of the world we own.
Ask why, and there is always an answer. Ask.

[Verse 3: Clarke Writes for Newton, 1715]
A chaplain in London answering for Newton:
space is a thing, he says, a stage God built,
absolute, empty, there before the motion.
I said: then show me which half of it tilts.
If the whole of it slid left, who could tell?

[Verse 4: Two Things Exactly Alike]
There are no two leaves in the garden the same.
Put two identical things in the world, and God
had no reason to put this one in that place —
and He does nothing without reason. So the odd
consequence is: identicals are one.

[Bridge: Man Gieng After Wege]
[Old High German charm, Ad Equum Errehet, for a lamed horse, tenth century]
Man gieng after wege, zoh sin ros in handoz,
do gisagita imo sin ros umbi sin fuoz.
A man went down the road and led his horse
by hand, and the horse told him of its foot.
Older than the quarrel. Still a limp.

[Chorus 3: Nihil Est Sine Ratione]

[Chorus 4: Five Letters, and Then Silence]
Five letters each, and then November came
and I was dead, and Clarke had the last word,
and printed both sides with his own name
on the cover, and the English all concurred.
Two hundred years. Then Mach. Then Einstein. Then me.

[Outro: Hanover, 14 November 1716]
No delegation from the academy I founded,
no word from London, no word from Berlin.
A secretary and a priest, and the ground
too hard that week, and the register thin.
Nothing is without reason. Even this.
