[Style: European symphonic, low strings and bassoon over a slow ostinato, industrial loom percussion, minor key with no resolution, bleak and relentless, andante pesante]
[Vocal: bass lead, close and exhausted, a low male chorus entering only on the choruses, no ornament]
[Language: English. One line of the German original in the Chorus, one line of Old High German in the Bridge.]

[Verse 1: Dean Street, Soho]
Two rooms above a tailor's, 1850,
six of us, and the landlady at the door.
Three of the children went before they were three:
Guido, Franziska, little Edgar. More
than I will write about. Jenny washed the sheets.

[Verse 2: The Reading Room, Seat G7]
Nine in the morning until seven at night
under the dome, every day for years.
Blue books, factory inspectors, the Poor Law,
the cotton returns, the testimony of the mills,
the exact hours worked by a child of nine.

[Chorus 1: Dead Labour]
Das Kapital ist verstorbene Arbeit —
capital is dead labour which, vampire-like,
lives only by sucking living labour,
and lives the more, the more of it it drinks.
Measure the day. The day is where it feeds.

[Verse 3: The Commodity]
Begin with the smallest thing: a single coat.
It has a use, and then it has a price,
and the price pretends to be a property
of cloth, as if the cloth had set it. Twice
removed from the woman who sewed the sleeve.

[Verse 4: Engels Paid for It]
Twenty years in a Manchester cotton office
he hated, sending money every month
to a man writing a book against the mill
that paid for both of them. The joke was his.
He said it first, and never once complained.

[Bridge: Unsar Trohtin Hat Farsalt]
[Old High German church song, the Petruslied, around 850, oldest German hymn]
Unsar trohtin hat farsalt sancte Petre giwalt,
daz er mag ginerian ze imo dingenten man.
Our Lord has handed over power to Peter.
The oldest German song is about who holds it,
and who may be let in, and on whose word.

[Chorus 3: Dead Labour]
Das Kapital ist verstorbene Arbeit —
capital is dead labour which, vampire-like,
lives only by sucking living labour,
and lives the more, the more of it it drinks.
Measure the day. The day is where it feeds.

[Chorus 4: Volume One, 1867]
A thousand copies. It sold slowly. One review.
Eighteen years of work and a German printer
and the second volume never came out — I knew
the shape of it and could not get the winter
out of my lungs. Engels finished it for me.

[Outro: Maitland Park Road, 14 March 1883]
Jenny died first, and then our daughter Jenny,
and then I had no reason left to argue.
He found me in the armchair in the afternoon.
Eleven people came. The ground was soft.
Measure the day. Somebody still is measuring.
