[Style: European symphonic march, massed orchestra and anvil percussion, minor-key brass chorale, workers unison chorus, rising and implacable, allegro risoluto]
[Vocal: bass-baritone lead answered by a large unison crowd chorus, open-throat, no ornament]
[Language: English. One line of Old High German incantation in the Bridge, one line of the German original in the Chorus.]

[Verse 1: Night Without an Edge]
In night without an edge the starlight shows
the shape of chimneys over Manchester.
My heart is set on where the morning goes.
The reading room is cold. The ledger's worse.
Somewhere a child is up and at the loom.

[Verse 2: The Voice of Labour]
The voice of labour carries to the sky
from Lancashire, Silesia, and the Rhine.
No longer will they bend, no longer lie
beneath a right that was not theirs to sign.
A spectre is haunting Europe. Let it walk.

[Chorus 1: Workers of All Countries]
Unite, and you are not alone again —
Proletarier aller Länder, vereinigt euch!
Working men of all countries, join your hands:
the future of the world is in your reach,
and nothing to lose but chains. So lose the chains.

[Verse 3: The Weight of History]
The weight of history is hope's own load;
we carry it uphill and do not stop.
For freedom, stubborn, up a broken road,
past every bayonet set at the top.
Men make their history — but not as they please.

[Verse 4: Thesis Eleven]
The philosophers have only read the world
in various ways. The point is to transform it.
I wrote that in a notebook, underlined,
and Engels found it years after I had gone.
Eleven theses. The eleventh is the one.

[Chorus 2: Workers of All Countries]
Unite, and you are not alone again —
Proletarier aller Länder, vereinigt euch!
Working men of all countries, join your hands:
the future of the world is in your reach,
and nothing to lose but chains. So lose the chains.

[Bridge: Insprinc Haptbandun]
[Old High German incantation, the First Merseburg Charm, tenth-century manuscript]
Insprinc haptbandun, inuar uigandun —
leap out of the fetters, go out from the foes.
Older than the Manifesto, older than
the very word for class, this was our German:
a charm for getting captive men from irons.

[Chorus 3: Workers of All Countries]
Unite, and you are not alone again —
Proletarier aller Länder, vereinigt euch!
Working men of all countries, join your hands:
the future of the world is in your reach,
and nothing to lose but chains. So lose the chains.

[Chorus 4: Out of the Rubble]
Out of the rubble, risen hand in hand,
tomorrow is a thing that gets composed.
Highgate is quiet. Only eleven stand
around the grave, and then the gate is closed,
and the ground trembles, and the light comes up.

[Outro: A New Movement]
The ground is trembling and the light breaks wide;
we are setting down a new movement now.
Not a prophecy. A score. A tide
that any century can learn to row.
Unite. The rest of it is arithmetic.
