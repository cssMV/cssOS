[Style: Cinematic score, solo cello and piano over slow swelling strings, Dutch folk fiddle in the bridge, nocturnal and surging, building from pianissimo to full orchestra, andante]
[Vocal: baritone lead, close and cracked, a wordless mixed choir rising on the choruses, no ornament]
[Language: English. One line of his own French in the Chorus, one line of the oldest Dutch in the Bridge.]

[Verse 1: Weak Light, Low Sky]
A weak light flickering, almost nothing there,
and the dark sky hanging low over the field.
Nuenen, winter, a potato-eater's chair,
five faces and one lamp. Nothing to yield.
I painted them brown because they were brown.

[Verse 2: The Yellow House, Arles, 1888]
Beneath the stars I sat and thought and thought,
and on the canvas everything was storming:
fifteen sunflowers in a jar I bought
for a man who was coming in the morning.
I put a bed in every room. He stayed nine weeks.

[Chorus 1: The Shining Dots]
Les points lumineux du firmament — why should
the shining dots of heaven be less near
than the black dots on the map of France?
I wander in the colours. The fire is here.
A train for the one, and death for the other. Go.

[Verse 3: Every Brushstroke Says It]
Every brushstroke is a sentence, and it says
the same thing in a hundred different ways.
A lonely shouting goes out through the days
and nobody writes back except my brother.
Eight hundred letters. Theo kept them all.

[Verse 4: Saint-Remy, June 1889]
A barred window, a cypress, and a sky
that would not stay still long enough to sit.
Eleven stars, a moon, a village lying
asleep under a church I made up for it.
I was in an asylum. I was never calmer.

[Chorus 2: The Shining Dots]
Les points lumineux du firmament — why should
the shining dots of heaven be less near
than the black dots on the map of France?
I wander in the colours. The fire is here.

[Bridge: Hebban Olla Vogala]
[Oldest Dutch sentence, written about 1100 in a Rochester manuscript]
Hebban olla vogala nestas hagunnan
hinase hic enda thu — wat unbidan we nu?
All the birds have begun their nests, except
for me and you. So what are we waiting for?
That was already Dutch. It was already this.

[Chorus 3: The Shining Dots]
Les points lumineux du firmament — why should
the shining dots of heaven be less near
than the black dots on the map of France?
I wander in the colours. The fire is here.
A train for the one, and death for the other. Go.

[Chorus 4: Auvers, July 1890]
Seventy days, seventy canvases, a field,
a crow, a path that goes three ways and stops.
Time stands completely still, and will not yield,
and the dream inside the painting comes back up.
Doctor Gachet was sadder than I was.

[Outro: Melting into Morning]
A weak light flickering, and then the slow
dissolving of it into the first grey.
La tristesse durera toujours, I said —
the sadness is going to last. Theo died
six months after me. They lie side by side.
