# EMPIRE OF TIME — Beat `b_converge`

**Thread:** earth · **Draft:** 1 · **2026-08-10**

> Built on `docs/timeempire-voyager-archive.md` v1.
> Entered from `b_earth_resist` **or** `b_mars_infiltrate` — both arrive here.
> Exits to `end_human_win` **or** `end_mars_control`.
>
> **The structural problem this beat had to solve, and how:**
> Rail 3 says the observer may only nudge — a hesitation, a glance, a word. But
> the graph's edges let the observer's attention decide which ending the film
> lands on. Those two things only coexist if **the fork is time.** Ninety
> minutes; four hundred and nine cards; no slack anywhere. A hesitation is not
> symbolic here. It costs cards. Nobody's plot changes, nobody betrays anybody,
> and the ending is still decided — by seconds.

---

## 1. NINETY MINUTES

**INT. DEEP SPACE NETWORK COMPLEX — TRANSMITTER ROOM — NIGHT**

Small, hot, ugly. Racks, a patch panel, one swivel chair, a gooseneck mic on a
desk that has cigarette burns older than anyone in the room.

Through the one window: the dish outside, slewing round, huge and unhurried.

> **BLOCKING:** keep the room *cramped*. Everything monumental in this beat is
> outside and unattended. Nobody stands heroically.

A wall clock. A carrier-lock lamp goes green.

ETHAN at the patch panel. CLARA in the chair with the shoebox in her lap and the
mic pulled down to her mouth.

**ETHAN**
Carrier's up. Twenty-one gigahertz, aimed and locked.
*(beat)*
Ninety minutes. It hands off to Madrid at 11:10 and there is no argument
available.

**CLARA**
How many cards.

**ETHAN**
Four hundred and nine.

**CLARA**
*(doing the arithmetic and not liking it)*
So thirteen seconds each.

**ETHAN**
Thirteen seconds each.

**CLARA**
Ethan, some of these people should get more than thirteen seconds.

**ETHAN**
*(quietly)*
Yes. They should.

She looks at the box.

**CLARA**
Then I'd better not stop.

She presses the key.

## 2. THE READING

**INT. TRANSMITTER ROOM — CONTINUOUS**

**CLARA**
*(into the mic, plainly, no performance)*
Marion Bell. Sixty-one. Drowned in her kitchen, ninth of April, holding the back
door shut against the water because her dog was still inside. The record says she
moved to Ohio.

She sets the card down. Takes the next.

**CLARA (CONT'D)**
Thomas Ansah. Nineteen. Died on the fourth floor of a building the record says
was demolished in 2019.

Next.

**CLARA (CONT'D)**
Unnamed. A woman, approximately thirty. No one reported her missing, because
the record says she was never issued a name.

> **SOUND (bible §九):** through this sequence the room tone is *full* — racks
> humming, air handling, the dish motor outside. Hold it. We are going to take
> it away.

**CUT TO:**

**EXT. DISH — CONTINUOUS**

The dish, from below, enormous, aimed at nothing visible.

We do not hear Clara out here. We hear wind, and the motor, and that is all.

> **NOTE:** the transmission is never visualised. No beam, no shimmer, no glow.
> The whole point is that it is already gone and there was nothing to see.

## 3. HE COMES ALONE

**INT. TRANSMITTER ROOM — 34 MINUTES IN**

The door opens.

xÈth. **Charcoal suit, no coat, desert dust on his shoes.** Behind him, JUNE
HALE, holding a folder she was told to bring, staying by the door.

No security. No weapon. Nobody moves to stop anything.

Ethan puts himself between xÈth and the chair. It is not brave; it is
instinctive, and slightly absurd, and xÈth allows it.

**xÈth**
Please don't stop on my account. I'd rather you kept going, actually — it's
easier to talk to somebody who's busy.

Clara does not stop. She takes the next card.

**CLARA**
*(into the mic)*
Ruth Ellery. Forty-four. The record says the fire started in her kitchen. It
started in the wall.

**xÈth**
*(to Ethan, conversational)*
Twenty-one gigahertz. That's the old uplink band.

**ETHAN**
You can't jam it.

**xÈth**
No.

**ETHAN**
You can't rewrite it.

**xÈth**
*(with something almost like admiration)*
No. That was well done. I have been at this a hundred and forty years and it
genuinely did not occur to me that anyone would simply *leave*.

## 4. THE MAP

He unrolls the register on the desk beside the shoebox. Coordinates. Two dates
in every row.

**xÈth**
Before you finish, I'd like you to see what you're aiming into.

Ethan looks. He knows what it is before he is told, and so does the audience.

**ETHAN**
How many.

**xÈth**
All of them.

**ETHAN**
And you're going to tell me something killed them.

**xÈth**
*(mildly)*
No, Mr. Vance. You've done the reading. You know what happened.

**ETHAN**
They stopped hurting.

**xÈth**
They stopped hurting. And then, quite soon afterwards, they stopped.

He rolls it up.

**xÈth (CONT'D)**
*(almost kindly)*
You've always assumed we came here to take something. **We came here because
you are still alive.**

**ETHAN**
Because we still hurt.

**xÈth**
*(a small nod — yes)*
Because you still hurt.

Ethan looks at the rolled-up chart, and then at him, and answers the whole film
in one line.

**ETHAN**
**Pain isn't the price of freedom. It's the mark it leaves.**

xÈth does not argue with it. That is the most frightening thing he does.

> **NOTE (bible §10 · 锁死台词):** both marked lines are verbatim from the
> bible and this is their only placement in the picture. Ethan's line is the
> English rendering the bible left to the screenplay — **it is now locked too.**
>
> **NOTE (bible §五):** twelve lines. Do not extend this. The map is evidence,
> not a lecture, and the film has already told the audience how to read it.

## 5. HIS CASE

**INT. TRANSMITTER ROOM — 58 MINUTES IN**

The cards are going down slower. Clara's voice has begun to do the thing voices
do after an hour of this.

xÈth crouches — not looming, *lower* than her — beside the chair.

> **SOUND:** from his first line here, pull the room. Racks out. Air handling
> out. Motor out. By the end of his speech the only sound in the film is two
> people breathing. This is the bible's 静音 母题 and it is the biggest use of it
> before the ending.

**xÈth**
Clara. May I say the unkind version? You've earned the unkind version.

**CLARA**
*(not looking up)*
Say it fast, I've got a hundred and forty left.

**xÈth**
He's doing this for his father.

Clara's hand stops on a card. One second. She picks it up anyway.

**xÈth (CONT'D)**
That's not a slur. It's the best reason a person ever has for anything. But it
is not the same as doing it for Marion Bell, and I think you knew that at about
card ninety.

**CLARA**
*(reading, steady)*
Peter Vasquez. Thirty-eight. The record says he —

**xÈth**
And you're doing it for him.

*(beat)*

**xÈth (CONT'D)**
Which leaves four hundred and nine people who are being carried tonight by
somebody who will not be able to put them down afterwards. I've watched people
do what you're doing. They don't recover. They become a kind of monument, and
monuments are not *alive*, Clara, they're just very slow.

Clara reads. Her voice is not entirely steady now.

**xÈth (CONT'D)**
I can take it off you. Not lie to you — I'd never insult you with that. I can
simply *settle* it. One number. One page. Everyone who is owed a name gets a
name, once, and it stays.
*(beat)*
And you get to go home and be a person.

**CLARA**
And the ones you settle wrong?

**xÈth**
Wrong once, in one place, where anyone can find it.

**CLARA**
And the ones you settle away?

He does not answer that, and his not answering is honest, and she registers the
honesty, which is worse.

**xÈth**
I'm not asking you to lie. I'm asking you to *stop*.
*(gently)*
That's all. Just stop. Nothing will happen. That's the whole offer — nothing
will happen, ever again.

## 6. THE PIVOT

**INT. TRANSMITTER ROOM — 71 MINUTES IN**

Silence, total — the sound is gone.

Clara, mid-card. The next card between two fingers, not yet lifted.

Ethan across the room, saying nothing, because he has understood that saying
anything would be worse.

June by the door, who believes in the Office, and who is nonetheless not able to
look at this.

The clock.

And under everything — **a heartbeat.** Hers. Slow. Unhurried. A little fast for
resting.

> **MOTIF · 心跳 2 of 3 (bible §九).** It is the same recording as the cold open.
> No one on screen can hear it. Nothing in the film ever explains it. The third
> and final use is the last four seconds of the picture.

**HOLD.**

> of dialogue and no plot event; the observer changes how long a woman looks at a
> piece of card.

## 7. HANDOFF TO THE ENDINGS

Both endings are separate beats and are not written here. What is fixed now,
and may not be altered by either of them:

1. **Neither branch ends on Earth.** Both cut, from wherever they are, to 2124 —
   Mars, the museum, the young Martian, the question, the answer, and then the
   record turning. Bible §7.
2. **The final four seconds are identical in both branches.** Same frame, same
   attitude, same black, same heartbeat (3 of 3). Bible §6.1. This is the single
   most important structural rule in the film: a viewer who plays both branches
   must find that the last thing they see did not move.
3. **The AI does not say "It was wrong."** Bible §7.1.
4. In `end_human_win` the receipt comes back **twenty-four hours later** and the
   film does not skip the wait. In `end_mars_control` there is nothing to wait
   for, and the silence of the following day is the point.
