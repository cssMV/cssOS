## 1. COLD OPEN — 1977

**FADE IN ON BLACK.**

Not silence. A *heartbeat*. Slow, unhurried, a little fast for resting.
It runs alone for a long time — long enough to become uncomfortable.

> **NOTE (motif · 心跳 1 of 3):** this is its first of three appearances in the
> entire film. Do not use it again until the bible says so.

**INT. LABORATORY, PASADENA — NIGHT — JUNE 1977**

Fluorescent tubes. Linoleum. A reel-to-reel deck turning.

A YOUNG WOMAN (late 20s, plain blouse, no jewellery) sits very still in a
straight-backed chair. Electrodes at her temples and her chest, taped down with
surgical tape. Wires run to a polygraph drum where four needles scratch.

A TECHNICIAN watches the drum, not her.

**TECHNICIAN**
Another twenty minutes.

**YOUNG WOMAN**
That's fine.

She closes her eyes. She is not meditating. She is *remembering something*, and
whatever it is, it is making her happy in a way that is difficult to watch.

The needles jump.

> **NOTE:** she is never named, on screen or in dialogue, in any cut of this film.
> The audience is not told what is being recorded. See bible §3.1 — this is
> structure, not exposition. Never point at it.

**CUT TO:**

**INT. ASSEMBLY CLEAN ROOM — DAY — JULY 1977**

White light. Men in white coats and paper caps around a bench. On the bench: a
gold-plated copper disc, twelve inches, in an open aluminium jacket.

At the back of the room, by the door, out of focus and never once framed: **a
man with close-cropped pale hair**, arms folded, watching with the mild interest
of someone waiting for a lift.

> **PLANT (pays off in `b_mars_infiltrate` §3):** this is xÈth. He is not
> identified, not named, not brought into focus, and no one speaks to him. On a
> first viewing the audience will not register him. On a second viewing he is
> unmistakable — and not a day younger. **Do not let the camera help.**

Four men are arguing. One of them — **THE FOURTH MAN**, 40s, still, the only one
not gesturing — is losing.

> **NOTE:** he is not named in this beat and no one addresses him by name. His
> identity is revealed in `b_earth_resist`. Frame him so his face is available
> but never emphasised — he is a man in a room, not a portrait.

**COMMITTEE MAN #1**
It's a greeting card. You don't put a funeral on a greeting card.

**COMMITTEE MAN #2**
Nobody's saying pretend. We're saying *select*. There's ninety minutes.
Ninety. You want to spend four of them on a woman crying in Georgia?

**THE FOURTH MAN**
Yes.

Silence. Somebody laughs, once, and stops.

**COMMITTEE MAN #1**
Whoever finds this — if anybody ever finds this — the first thing they
learn about us shouldn't be that we hurt.

**THE FOURTH MAN**
It should be the second thing.

He picks up the disc. Handles it the way you handle something already old.

**THE FOURTH MAN (CONT'D)**
Put in the thunder. Put in the whales. Put in the mother, and the war song, and
the funeral. Put in all of it.

**COMMITTEE MAN #2**
Why?

**THE FOURTH MAN**
*(simply)*
Because someone is going to try to take it out.

Beat.

From the back of the room, by the door — **a single quiet laugh.** Genuinely
amused. Warm, even.

It gives the others permission. They look at each other, and the room relaxes,
and the thing that was just said is filed, permanently, as a joke.

**CUT TO:**

**INT. CLEAN ROOM — CONTINUOUS**

CLOSE ON the aluminium jacket. A technician's gloved hands lower a thin plated
layer into place along the cover.

**TECHNICIAN (V.O.)**
Uranium two-thirty-eight. Electroplated. Half-life's four and a half billion
years — so whoever picks it up can measure what's left and work out how long
ago we sent it.

**THE FOURTH MAN (V.O.)**
*(quietly, off)*
A clock nobody can argue with.

The cover closes over the disc.

**HARD CUT TO BLACK.**

**TITLE:**

> **EMPIRE OF TIME**

## 2. THE OBSERVATION STATION — PRESENT

**INT. TIME OBSERVATION STATION — BASEMENT LEVEL — DAY**

Not a laboratory. A **records room**. Grey steel shelving to the ceiling, paper
in boxes, three computer terminals from different decades, a kettle. A window
well at ceiling height shows shoes walking past on a pavement.

> **WARDROBE / ERA (mandatory per bible §10.5 铁律 6):** near-future, but
> *unglamorous* — Ethan in a worn charcoal crewneck and canvas jacket. No
> tactical gear. No armour. No visible future-tech. If it looks like science
> fiction, it is wrong.

ETHAN VANCE (early 30s, thin vertical scar through the left eyebrow, permanently
tired) stands at a long table with two copies of the same government report open
side by side.

He reads the left one. He reads the right one. He goes back to the left one.

**ETHAN**
*(to himself)*
No. No, no, no.

He photographs both pages with a cheap camera. Writes the date on a card in
pencil. Puts the card in a shoebox with about four hundred other cards.

A colleague, MARGOT (50s, cardigan, kind, oblivious), leans in the doorway with
two mugs.

**MARGOT**
You've been down here since Tuesday.

**ETHAN**
Margot — the '09 flood. How many died?

**MARGOT**
*(no hesitation)*
Two hundred and eleven.

**ETHAN**
You're sure.

**MARGOT**
My cousin was one of them. Yes, I'm sure.

Ethan turns the right-hand report around so she can read it.

**MARGOT (CONT'D)**
*(reading)*
"…one hundred and forty-nine confirmed fatalities."
*(beat)*
Well, that's a typo.

**ETHAN**
It's in all of them. Every copy. The federal record, the state record, the
newspaper microfilm, the memorial plaque. I drove out and looked at the plaque.

**MARGOT**
*(smiling, patient)*
Then it's a hundred and forty-nine, love. And I misremembered.

She says it easily. She *believes* it, in real time, while we watch. Something
in her face has already smoothed over.

She takes her mug and goes.

Ethan stands very still.

> **NOTE (核心机制 · bible §2.1):** this is the entire horror of the film and it
> must land here, in the first ten minutes, with no science fiction attached.
> They do not travel through time. They **edit**, and the edit reaches into the
> living. Margot is not lying. Margot has been rewritten.

**ETHAN**
*(after a long moment, to the empty room)*
She was sure.

## 3. THE THING THAT CANNOT BE ARGUED WITH

**INT. OBSERVATION STATION — SIDE ROOM — CONTINUOUS**

A smaller room. On a steel bench: a lead-lined case. Ethan opens it.

Inside, in foam: a small dull metal coupon, and beside it a scintillation counter
that looks thirty years old.

He clips the coupon into the counter. Presses a key.

The counter ticks. Slowly. Irregularly. He watches the number climb.

**ETHAN (V.O.)**
Everything they can reach is a *record*. Paper, film, a database, a plaque, a
person. All of it can be revised, and the revision is perfect, because they also
revise everyone who could tell you it changed.

The counter settles on a figure. He writes it in a notebook, next to forty
identical previous entries.

**ETHAN (V.O.) (CONT'D)**
So you stop asking documents. You ask metal.

He compares the notebook figure against a printed table.

**ETHAN (V.O.) (CONT'D)**
This coupon was cast on a known day. Every second since then, a fixed fraction of
it has stopped being what it was. Nobody has a pen for that.

He closes the notebook.

**ETHAN (V.O.) (CONT'D)**
Which is how I know the calendar is wrong by nine days, and has been for a
century, and I am the only one in this building who can see it.

*(beat)*

**ETHAN (V.O.) (CONT'D)**
There are two others.

## 4. CLARA

**INT. OBSERVATION STATION — STAIRWELL — LATER**

CLARA SINCLAIR (early 30s, copper hair pinned back, a small dark mole below the
right eye, a coat she has clearly slept in) comes down two steps at a time. She
does not knock, because she never has.

> **WARDROBE:** civilian, practical, slightly rumpled. She is not an agent.

**CLARA**
Get your coat.

**ETHAN**
Clara —

**CLARA**
The Office of Remembrance filed at four this morning. Not a bill. An
*administrative rule*. They don't need anybody's permission for an
administrative rule.

**ETHAN**
Filed *what*.

**CLARA**
Consolidation. Every federal archive, every state record, every school text,
into one authoritative index. For consistency.
*(beat)*
Ethan, they're not hiding it. It's on the public register. It has a **logo**.

Ethan sits down slowly on the stairs.

**ETHAN**
That's not an attack.

**CLARA**
It's worse. It's *tidying*.

She sits beside him. Their shoulders touch. Neither moves.

**CLARA (CONT'D)**
*(not looking at him)*
When it's finished there'll be one copy of everything. And one copy is one thing
to correct.

**ETHAN**
Then we correct it back.

**CLARA**
With what? Our memory? We're three people, and one of us —
*(she stops)*

**ETHAN**
Say it.

**CLARA**
One of us is half theirs.

A silence that is very old between them.

**ETHAN**
*(evenly)*
Yes.

**CLARA**
That wasn't —

**ETHAN**
It was true. It's allowed to be true.

She turns, and takes his face in both hands, roughly, the way you grab something
before it goes over an edge.

**CLARA**
I swore I'd hold the line. I did that before I knew you. If it comes to the line
or you, I'm going to pick the line, and you're going to *let me*.

**ETHAN**
I know.

**CLARA**
Say you know.

**ETHAN**
I know.

She lets go. Neither of them has moved from the step.

> **NOTE (bible §3.1):** Clara's oath is the Druyan hour in a person. She is
> guarding, in herself, the capacity to be hurt. Do not have anyone say this.
> Not in this beat, not in any beat.

## 5. THE ENVOY

**INT. FEDERAL PRESS ROOM — DAY**

A podium. Not grand — a working room, blue drapes, a dozen chairs, half of them
empty. This is deliberately *small*: he is not yet where he will be.

xÈth (ageless, close-cropped silver-white hair, unlined, pale amber eyes) steps
up. **Charcoal suit, plain tie, no flag pin, no insignia.** He does not use
notes.

> **HARD RED LINE (bible §10.5 铁律 5):** xÈth is an original synthetic face.
> He must not resemble any real, living or historical public figure. This applies
> to casting reference, prompt, and every generated frame.

**xÈth**
Good morning. This will be short.

*(he actually waits until the room is quiet — a small, disarming courtesy)*

**xÈth (CONT'D)**
Last year the Department published two different figures for the same flood.
Two. For the same water, on the same day, on the same river.

A ripple of recognition. Somebody nods.

**xÈth (CONT'D)**
A woman in that county has spent eleven years being told, by her own government,
two incompatible things about the worst morning of her life. She has had to
carry both. That is not an administrative problem. That is a *cruelty*, and we
committed it by accident, through nothing worse than untidiness.

He lets that sit. He is very good at this.

**xÈth (CONT'D)**
So: one record. One number. Checked, and settled, and *finished* — so that she
can put it down.

**REPORTER (O.S.)**
And if the settled number is wrong?

**xÈth**
*(without a flicker)*
Then it will be wrong once, in one place, where anyone can find it and say so.
*(beat)*
At the moment it is wrong in nine hundred places and no one can say anything at
all.

He steps back from the podium. Then, almost as an afterthought, he leans back in.

**xÈth (CONT'D)**
I'm told there are people who find this frightening. I'd ask them a question, and
I'd ask it kindly.
*(beat)*
What exactly do you think we're protecting, when we protect a memory of being
hurt?

He leaves. The room does not know whether to applaud.

> **NOTE (bible §四 · 节点二):** he must win this scene. If any part of the
> audience is not, briefly, on his side, the third act has no weight. Direct him
> as decent. He believes it.

## 6. THE BOMB, SHOWN

**INT. OBSERVATION STATION — RECORDS ROOM — NIGHT**

Rain in the window well. Ethan alone with a projector throwing an old NASA frame
on the wall — a spacecraft, high-gain dish, booms out, against nothing.

Clara comes in with two coffees, stops, watches him watch it.

**CLARA**
You've been on that for an hour.

**ETHAN**
The consolidation swept up a set of minutes from 1977. Four men arguing about
what to put on a phonograph record.

**CLARA**
*(not following)*
And?

**ETHAN**
One of them wanted the funerals left in. The thunder, the war song, a woman
crying. The others thought it was a greeting card.
*(beat)*
He lost the argument in the room. But I pulled the contents list.
*(beat)*
It's all in there. Every last unbearable second of it.

**CLARA**
So he lost and won.

**ETHAN**
Somebody made sure that record was *honest*. Fifty years before anybody needed
it to be.

He turns the projector so the frame falls across her.

**ETHAN (CONT'D)**
It's a copper disc. It isn't data — it's a *shape*. A groove. You can't revise a
groove from here; you'd have to go and put your hands on it.

**CLARA**
So we go and —

**ETHAN**
It's twenty-five billion kilometres away and it is not slowing down.

He says the next thing quietly, because he has just understood it.

**ETHAN (CONT'D)**
**There is one copy of Earth they cannot rewrite.**

Clara looks at the wall. At the small bright thing on it.

**CLARA**
How far.

**ETHAN**
Light takes a day to reach it. A day back. Seventeen kilometres every second,
further, for the rest of time.
*(beat)*
And there's a layer of uranium on the cover, so whoever finds it can work out
exactly when we sent it. Even *that* can't be argued with.

**CLARA**
Then it's already safe.

**ETHAN**
It's already *gone*. That's not the same thing.

**CLARA**
What's the difference?

**ETHAN**
*(looking at her)*
It only says what we put in it in 1977. It can't hear us now.

> **NOTE (bible §八 · 明牌不揭秘):** the audience now knows everything Ethan
> knows. From here every scene is a clock, not a puzzle. Under no circumstances
> re-hide this later for a third-act reveal.

## 7. THE FORK

**INT. OBSERVATION STATION — RECORDS ROOM — CONTINUOUS**

Two things are happening in the same room.

On the wall: the projected spacecraft. Ethan in front of it, working, pulling
boxes, already at it — resistance, without a plan.

On the small terminal in the corner, muted, on a loop: xÈth at the podium, that
last question, over and over, silent.

Clara stands between the two lights, and looks at neither of them, and waits.

**CLARA**
*(without turning)*
Whatever we're going to do, we should decide now — while we can still tell it
was us who decided.
