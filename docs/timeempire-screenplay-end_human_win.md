## 1. UNDER A MINUTE

**INT. TRANSMITTER ROOM — 89 MINUTES IN**

Clara's voice is wrecked. She is not slowing down.

**CLARA**
*(into the mic)*
Ilse Brandt. Twenty-two. The record says she was never admitted.

She sets the card down. The box is empty.

She looks into it anyway, the way you check a pocket.

**ETHAN**
*(reading the clock, very quietly)*
Fifty-one seconds.

Nobody says anything for fifty-one seconds. It is a long time on screen and it
should be left alone.

The carrier lamp goes amber. Then out.

**ETHAN (CONT'D)**
Madrid has it.

## 2. HE DOES THE ARITHMETIC

**INT. TRANSMITTER ROOM — CONTINUOUS**

xÈth has not moved from beside the chair the entire time.

He stands. Straightens his jacket. He is not angry, and the absence of anger is
the most unsettling thing he does in the film.

**ETHAN**
*(braced)*
You're not going to do anything.

**xÈth**
No.

**ETHAN**
You could arrest us. You could correct the report. You could —

**xÈth**
Mr. Vance.
*(patiently, as if to a colleague)*
What was I building?

Ethan doesn't answer.

**xÈth (CONT'D)**
One record. One number. **Settled.** That was the whole of it — not a lie, not a
cage. Just: *this is the figure, and it is the only figure, and now you may put
it down.*

He looks at the empty shoebox.

**xÈth (CONT'D)**
There is now a second copy. It is accurate, it is complete, it is unreachable,
and it is leaving at the speed of light in a straight line forever.
*(beat)*
I can still settle every record on this planet. It will simply never again be
*the only one*.

**CLARA**
*(hoarse, not turning round)*
So it's finished.

**xÈth**
*(genuinely)*
It was finished at nine forty-one.

> **NOTE:** he is not defeated by heroism, by exposure, or by force. He is
> defeated by **arithmetic**. His project required uniqueness. Ninety minutes of
> a tired woman reading names into a dish made uniqueness permanently
> unavailable. Play the scene as a man closing a file.

He goes to the door. JUNE is there. She has still not spoken and does not speak
now; she opens the door for him, and follows him out, and that is her answer.

> **JUNE (per Jing, 2026-08-10):** no line. Not here, not anywhere in the film.
> She is not converted and she is not punished. She simply still believes he was
> being kind, and she leaves with him, and the film lets her.

## 3. THE WAIT

**EXT. DISH — DAWN**

The dish, idle, tipped over toward nothing. Two people asleep in a car at the
fence line.

**CAPTION:** *Twenty-four hours out.*

**INT. CLARA'S FLAT — KITCHEN — NIGHT**

Radio on low. Neither of them is listening to it. Clara has the empty shoebox on
the table in front of her and has not put it away.

**ETHAN**
You should sleep.

**CLARA**
I keep getting to a name and then finding out I've already said it.

She turns the box round. Round again.

**CLARA (CONT'D)**
He was right about that part. Wasn't he.

**ETHAN**
Yes.

**CLARA**
*(a small, wrecked laugh)*
You could lie.

**ETHAN**
Not to you. That's the entire point of me.

> **NOTE (bible §3.1):** the cost is real and permanent and the film does not
> cure it. Clara is carrying four hundred and nine people. Nobody in any
> remaining scene tells her it was worth it.

**CAPTION:** *Twenty-four hours back.*

> **NOTE:** do not compress this. Two captions, real screen time between them,
> a day of ordinary life. The delay is the metronome the film has been running
> since `b_earth_resist` and skipping it to reach the payoff faster would be a
> lie about physics and a lie about grief.

## 4. THE RECEIPT

**INT. TRANSMITTER ROOM — NIGHT — FORTY-EIGHT HOURS LATER**

Ethan alone at the patch panel. Clara in the doorway with two coffees, not
coming in.

A speaker. Hiss. Nothing.

Hiss.

Then — a tone. Flat, mechanical, unmusical. It lasts about a second and a half
and it is the least dramatic sound in the film.

Ethan puts one hand flat on the desk.

**ETHAN**
That's it. That's the whole answer. It's just — *received*.

**CLARA**
It heard us.

**ETHAN**
It heard us two days ago.
*(beat)*
It's a day and a half further away than that now.

Clara comes in. Puts a coffee down. Stands behind his chair with a hand on his
shoulder, and neither of them says the obvious thing, which is that nothing
about the world outside this room has changed at all.

**ETHAN (CONT'D)**
*(finally)*
Nobody's ever going to know we did this.

**CLARA**
Somebody already does.

She isn't talking about a person, and she isn't being mystical. She means the
light.

## 5. OUT

**INT. OBSERVATION STATION — RECORDS ROOM — DAY**

Empty shelves. Consolidation finished. It looks like any office anyone ever
cleared out.

MARGOT is packing a mug into a box. She looks up, friendly.

**MARGOT**
Two hundred and eleven.

Ethan stops.

**MARGOT (CONT'D)**
*(puzzled at herself, half-laughing)*
Sorry. I don't know why I said that. It's been in my head all week.

She goes back to packing.

> **NOTE:** she has not remembered. Nothing has been restored to her. The film
> is not offering a repair. She has simply — once — said a true number out loud,
> for no reason she can identify, in a building where the true number no longer
> exists anywhere.
>
> Ethan does not correct her, does not explain, does not react beyond stopping.
> **This is the last dialogue on Earth in this branch.**

Ethan picks up his box. Goes out.
