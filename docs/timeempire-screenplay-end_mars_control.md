## 1. FOUR SECONDS

**INT. TRANSMITTER ROOM — 71 MINUTES IN**

Clara, the card between two fingers, not yet lifted.

She sets it down on the desk.

Not in surrender. She just — puts it down, and closes her eyes, and breathes,
because she has been talking for an hour and eleven minutes and she is a person.

**One. Two. Three. Four.**

She picks it up again.

**CLARA**
*(into the mic, going on)*
Hannah Weiss. Fifty-two.

> **NOTE — THIS IS THE ENTIRE FORK AND IT MUST NOT BE DRAMATISED.** She does not
> falter morally. She does not choose xÈth. She does not look at him. She rests
> for four seconds at the eighth hour of the worst night of her life, which is
> the most forgivable thing anyone does in this film.
>
> The audience must not, on first viewing, understand that anything has just
> happened. Direct these four seconds as nothing.

## 2. ELEVEN-TEN

**INT. TRANSMITTER ROOM — 90 MINUTES IN**

**CLARA**
*(fast now, hoarse)*
Sof—

The carrier lamp goes amber.

She does not get the rest of the name out. Neither do we.

**ETHAN**
*(already moving)*
No — no, hold it, *hold it* —

Out. The lamp is out. Outside, audible through the wall, the dish motor starts
up and begins, patiently, to slew away toward something else.

Clara is still holding the card.

**ETHAN (CONT'D)**
*(into a handset)*
This is DSS-14, we need — can you extend, we need four minutes —

He listens.

He puts the handset down.

**CLARA**
How many.

**ETHAN**
Clara —

**CLARA**
*(not a question)*
**How many.**

He looks in the box.

**ETHAN**
Three.

## 3. THREE

**INT. TRANSMITTER ROOM — CONTINUOUS**

Nobody moves.

Clara reads the three cards to herself, silently, in the dead room, because
there is nothing else to do with them. We do not hear the names.

She puts them back in the box and puts the lid on.

> **NOTE:** the film never tells us who those three were. Not here, not in the
> coda, not in any supplementary material, ever. **The whole point of them is
> that nobody will ever know.**
>
> **Arithmetic (checked):** she loses four seconds at 71 minutes, which is three
> cards. She therefore cannot be cut off on the last card — she is cut off on
> **407**, one syllable in. Cards 407, 408 and 409 stay in the box.
>
> In `end_human_win` card 409 is Ilse Brandt, and she gets out with fifty-one
> seconds to spare. A viewer who plays both branches may work out that Ilse
> Brandt is one of the three. **The film still never says so** — and the fact
> that the last woman to make it in one world is among the lost in the other is
> the best argument for playing it twice. Do not add a line pointing at this.

xÈth has not gloated, has not spoken, has not moved. When Clara finally looks up
at him, he is looking at the box, and what is on his face is not triumph.

It is something much worse: **sympathy.**

**xÈth**
*(gently)*
It was ninety minutes and four hundred and nine people. Nobody could have done
that.

**CLARA**
Somebody nearly did.

**xÈth**
Yes.

He says it with complete respect, and then he goes, and JUNE opens the door for
him and follows him out without a word.

> **JUNE (per Jing, 2026-08-10):** no line, in this branch or any other. She
> holds the door. She still believes he was being kind. The film does not punish
> her for it and does not vindicate her either.

## 4. NOBODY COMES

**INT. CLARA'S FLAT — KITCHEN — DAY**

Sunlight. Radio on low. The shoebox on the table with the lid on.

Ethan at the window, watching the street. Watching it the way you watch a street
when you are waiting for cars that do not arrive.

**CLARA**
It's been eleven days.

**ETHAN**
I know.

**CLARA**
Nobody's come. No arrest. No correction. My job's still my job. Your station's
still —
*(beat)*
Ethan, why hasn't anything *happened*?

**ETHAN**
*(without turning)*
Because we're not a problem.

He finally turns round.

**ETHAN (CONT'D)**
Four hundred and six people went out. Nobody heard them. Nobody was listening,
nobody was ever going to be listening, and in nine hundred years the light will
still be going and it will still be true and it will still not have *done*
anything.
*(beat)*
We didn't lose to him. We just weren't relevant.

> **NOTE (bible §九 · 静音):** from here to the end of this branch, take a layer
> of ambient out of every scene. By §6 there should be almost nothing under the
> dialogue. The world is not becoming oppressive. It is becoming *quiet*.

## 5. THE FIRST YEAR

**INT. OBSERVATION STATION — RECORDS ROOM — DAY**

Empty shelves. Clean. Someone has painted.

MARGOT, packing a mug into a box, cheerful.

**MARGOT**
There you are. They're giving us Fridays, did you hear? Whole department.
*(beat)*
It's a good scheme, Ethan. Honestly, whatever you think of them. My sister's
sleeping again.

**ETHAN**
That's good.

**MARGOT**
The flood thing finally got settled, as well. One number, and that's that. Went
in the paper and everything.

**ETHAN**
*(carefully)*
And what was it?

**MARGOT**
*(easily, without a flicker)*
A hundred and forty-nine.
*(beat)*
God, that was a bad year.

She takes her box and goes, and she is content, and she is not lying, and there
is now no one and nothing on this planet that disagrees with her.

Ethan stands in the empty room.

> **NOTE:** this is the darkest beat in the film and it must be played *warm*.
> Margot is happier than we have ever seen her. Her sister is sleeping. That is
> not sarcasm — it is the actual result, and it is genuinely good, and it cost
> everything. If the scene plays as dystopia it has failed.

## 6. THE DECLARATION

**INT. CLARA'S FLAT — LIVING ROOM — NIGHT**

The television on with the sound almost down. On it: a hall, a podium, xÈth.
Behind him a date, and the words **YEAR ONE.**

He is speaking. We do not hear a word of it.

Clara is asleep on the sofa. Ethan sits on the floor beside her with the shoebox
on his knees, lid off, looking at three cards.

He reads them. Silently. We still do not hear the names.

He puts the lid back on.

> **SOUND:** at this point the ambient bed is gone entirely. Television inaudible.
> No score. Room tone only — and then, for the last few seconds before the cut,
> not even that.
