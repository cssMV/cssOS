### Islamic — nasheed only; red-lines enforced
- **Tala' al-Badru 'Alayna**: the only plausibly-PD melody, but every available
  notation is a modern crowd-sourced MuseScore/Flat.io arrangement carrying the
  arranger's copyright. Per the doc + red-line, this must be **hand-transcribed**
  from the ancient melody, not downloaded. **NOT DOWNLOADED** — no clean PD
  notation file exists to fetch.
- **Qur'anic recitation (tajwīd/tilāwa) and the Adhan: categorically excluded.**
  Not music, not searched, not ingested. (Hard red-line.)

### Buddhist — **notation-blocked.** Shōmyō / Tibetan yang / Chinese fanbai are
neumatic memory-aid notation, not pitch-exact; pitch-exact transcriptions live
only in copyrighted ethnomusicology monographs. No PD machine-readable notation.
**Nothing downloaded.**

### Hindu — **largely oral.** Rabindra Sangeet is PD as composition but published
only in *swaralipi* (Bengali cipher) PDF scans — no clean MusicXML corpus.
Traditional bhajans are oral; any staff/harmonium transcription carries an
arranger's copyright. No clean PD machine-readable notation. **Nothing downloaded.**

### Sikh — **notation-blocked.** Gurbani kirtan is oral/discipular; the one
landmark notated collection (*Gurbani Sangeet: Prachin Reet Ratnavali*) is a
modern copyrighted academic work. No PD notation. **Nothing downloaded.**

### Taoist / Confucian — **notation-blocked.** Yayue and Taoist ritual chant
survive in gongche (工尺谱), needing manual conversion; pitch-exact modern
transcriptions are copyrighted. The one lead — **Jiang Kui, *Baishidaoren Gequ*
(1202)** — has only scholarly decipherments whose transcription copyright is
uncertain (skip when in doubt). No clean PD MusicXML. **Nothing downloaded.**

### Baháʼí — **not a PD source.** The faith is modern (1844+); texts,
authoritative translations, and essentially all musical settings are under active
copyright. No PD notation corpus exists. **Nothing downloaded.**
